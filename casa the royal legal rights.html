Gambling & Gaming Laws

Operating a commercial Airbnb villa equipped with casino-style games (like poker or blackjack) involves strict legal boundaries under Indian central and state laws. Operating casino games in a private villa for commercial purposes or profit is illegal in almost all parts of India, except in states/union territories with specialized licensing — Goa, Sikkim, and Daman.

Critical Legal Warning

Casa The Royal does NOT operate, promote, or facilitate any form of real-money gambling, casino games, or wagering activities on its premises. Any such activity by guests is solely their personal responsibility and is neither endorsed nor permitted by the property management.

Section 3 — Penalty for Owning/Keeping a Common Gaming House

Public Gambling Act, 1867 & Maharashtra Prevention of Gambling Act, 1887

It is a criminal offense to open, keep, or use any house, room, or space as a "common gaming house." If a property charges an entry fee, takes a commission or house rake, or charges extra rent for providing poker or blackjack tables, the premises is classified as a common gaming house.

Public Gambling Act, 1867 — Section 3: "Whoever keeps or uses any house, room, tent, enclosure or place for the purpose of gaming… shall be punishable with imprisonment… and with a fine."

Section 4 — Penalty for Being Found in a Common Gaming House

Public Gambling Act, 1867

Anyone found playing games of chance or present in such a premises for the purpose of gaming can be arrested and fined. This applies to both the operator and the guests present on the premises.

Public Gambling Act, 1867 — Section 4: "Whoever is found in any such house, room, tent, enclosure or place… shall be punishable with fine not exceeding one hundred rupees, or with imprisonment…"

Section 12 — Games of Mere Skill Exemption

Public Gambling Act, 1867

The law exempts games of "mere skill." While courts have debated the classification of poker, Blackjack is legally categorized as a game of chance (house-edge casino game) across all Indian jurisdictions. Running live Blackjack with money or chips on the premises directly violates Section 3.

Public Gambling Act, 1867 — Section 12: "Nothing in this Act shall apply to any game of mere skill wherever played."

Section 13 — Public Gambling / Playing in Public Places

Public Gambling Act, 1867

If the villa host hosts public events or advertises "casino nights" open to the general public, it constitutes illegal public gaming under this section, regardless of whether the venue is technically a private property.

Public Gambling Act, 1867 — Section 13: Prohibits gaming in any public place, street, or thoroughfare.
Illegal
  • Offering professional poker/blackjack setups
  • Providing physical casino tables for wagering
  • Charging money for gambling access in an Airbnb
  • Advertising "casino nights" to the public
Permitted
  • Friends playing casual board games
  • No real-money wagering involved
  • No house commissions or entry fees for games
  • Standard board games (Chess, Monopoly, Carrom)

Crucial Distinction: Friends playing casual board games without real-money wagering or house commissions is generally ignored by authorities, but offering professional poker/blackjack setups, physical tables, or charging money for gambling access in an Airbnb is explicitly illegal.

Compliance Required

Airbnb Villa & Property Operations Laws

Running a short-term rental villa requires compliance with commercial, local municipal, and safety regulations as outlined below.

Registration & Local Licenses

Mandatory for Commercial Villa Operations

State Tourism / Homestay Registration

Many state governments (e.g., Maharashtra Tourism Development Corporation / MTDC Homestay Scheme) require mandatory registration of villas and homestays operating as short-term rentals.

Local Municipal Corporation / Shops & Establishments Act

Operating a villa as a commercial business without a Trade License or Commercial Building NOC can lead to penalties under local municipal acts.

Guest Record-Keeping & Public Safety

Mandatory Identity Verification

Registration of Foreigners Rules, 1992 (Form C)

Under Section 5 of the Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939, hosts MUST record foreign national details and submit Form C on the FRRO portal within 24 hours of check-in.

Local Police Verification (Form B)

Hosts must maintain a guest register (Form B) recording Government ID proofs (Aadhaar, Passport) for all Indian nationals. Failure to comply violates Section 188 of the Indian Penal Code / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) for disobeying a public servant's order.

Noise & Public Nuisance Laws

Noise Pollution Rules, 2000

Rule 5 of the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 restricts high-decibel music and loudspeakers after 10:00 PM. Violations invite police action and property sealings under Section 268/290 of the IPC (Public Nuisance).

Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 — Rule 5: Restricts use of loudspeakers/public address systems after 10:00 PM except in closed premises for specific purposes.

Tax Compliance

Income Tax & GST Obligations

Income Tax Act, 1961 — Section 194-O

Airbnb deducts TCS/TDS on host earnings. All income from short-term rental operations must be declared under applicable income tax provisions.

Central GST Act, 2017

If total annual business turnover crosses ₹20 Lakhs (₹10 Lakhs in special category states), mandatory GST registration and tax remittance apply.

Action Items

Key Recommendations

Remove Real-Money Casino Setups

Do not market, charge fees for, or provide casino tables (especially Blackjack or real-money Poker) at the property to prevent severe criminal liability under the Public Gambling Act, 1867.

Stick to Casual Board Games

Provide standard board games (Chess, Monopoly, Carrom, Ludo) that carry zero legal risk and enhance the guest experience without any regulatory concerns.

Register the Property

Complete state tourism homestay registration, obtain local panchayat/municipal approvals, and ensure all commercial operating licenses are in place and renewed on time.

Verify All Guest IDs

Maintain a strict guest register (Form B for Indians, Form C for foreigners submitted on FRRO portal within 24 hours) at all times. This is non-negotiable legal compliance.

Enforce Noise Curfew at 10 PM

Communicate the 10:00 PM noise restriction to all guests at check-in. Include it in the house rules on the Airbnb listing to avoid police complaints and potential property sealing.
